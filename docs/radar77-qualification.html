Package qualification and physical analysis

QualificationPackage

Before an automotive chip ships, its package and die must survive accelerated stress that stands in for 15 years in a car. AEC-Q100 sets the default tests, conditions and sample sizes; the customer's mission profile can require more. After stress, parts are tested electrically and also opened up: acoustic microscopy, X-ray and cross-sections show whether the package is still sound inside even when it still passes electrically. The physics of the failure mechanisms is on the reliability page; how the package is built is on the assembly page.

Setting the conditions: from mission profile to test

  1. Start from the mission profile: temperatures, humidity, power cycles and on-time over 15 years (mission profile).
  2. Pick the acceleration model per mechanism: Coffin–Manson for temperature cycling (solder and crack fatigue), Peck for humidity (corrosion), Arrhenius (plus voltage) for HTOL and HTSL.
  3. Compute the equivalent test duration and compare with the AEC-Q100 default; use the larger. If it is far larger, consider a more severe condition or a tailored robustness validation test.
  4. Do not exceed the physics: test temperatures must stay below material limits (mold compound Tg, solder melting, maximum junction rating), or new failure mechanisms appear that never happen in the field.
  5. Define read-points and physical checks in the qualification plan before starting, and the criteria for passing.
Temperature cycling (Coffin–Manson): N_test = N_field · (ΔT_field / ΔT_test)^m m ≈ 2–2.5 (SAC solder), 4–6 (brittle cracks) (Norris–Landzberg adds cycle-frequency and T_max terms for solder) Humidity (Peck): AF = (RH_test / RH_use)^n · exp[ (Ea/k)·(1/T_use − 1/T_test) ] n ≈ 2.7–3, Ea ≈ 0.79 eV High temperature (Arrhenius): AF = exp[ (Ea/k)·(1/T_use − 1/T_test) ] (calculator)

  • Cross-section preparation: the part is potted in epoxy, cut near the row of interest, ground and polished down to the exact ball, bump or via; ion milling removes polishing smear for fine features. Pick the row with the highest strain (corner balls, die-edge RDL).
  • Measure, don't only look: crack length as a percentage of the joint diameter, IMC thickness, void area, layer thickness. Compare with time-zero samples from the same lot.
  • Sampling: passing parts are also sectioned (typically 2–5 per stress leg), because a crack that is 60 % through a ball passes the electrical test today and fails in the car later.

When a part fails

  1. Electrical verification: confirm the fail on the tester and on the bench; isolate the pin or block (bench debug).
  2. Non-destructive: X-ray, CSAM, thermal imaging to locate the defect.
  3. Destructive: decap or cross-section through the located spot; SEM/EDX for structure and chemistry.
  4. Root cause and corrective action (8D), and a decision whether the qualification must be repeated (8D).

Process and material changes are notified to automotive customers (PCN) and qualified per the AEC-Q100 change matrix before parts with the change ship. For modules, AEC-Q104 and the OEM's board-level tests (temperature cycling of the soldered board, vibration, mechanical shock) are added (module page).

Values are typical for 77 GHz automotive radar SoCs and meant for planning; check your process, product and customer requirements.
