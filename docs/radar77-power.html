Power management: supplies, LDOs and UV/OV monitors

SupplySafety

A radar SoC runs a 20 GHz VCO that turns microvolts of supply noise into FM, PAs that switch hundreds of mA every chirp, precise IF/ADC circuits and a large digital core, all from a 12 V car battery through an external PMIC. The on-chip power system has three jobs: regulate each domain cleanly (LDOs and the bandgap reference), sequence start-up and shutdown safely, and monitor every rail so that any under- or over-voltage is caught before it corrupts radar data (an ISO 26262 safety mechanism). The overview is in the signal-chain power section; this page goes into the circuits.

Bandgap reference

Every LDO and every UV/OV threshold is only as accurate as its reference. A bandgap adds a voltage that falls with temperature (a diode's V_BE, about −2 mV/°C) to one that rises with it (a scaled ΔV_BE of two diodes at different current densities, proportional to absolute temperature) so the sum is nearly temperature-independent:

V_BG = V_BE + K · (kT/q) · ln(N) ≈ 1.2 V (CTAT + PTAT) Accuracy after trim: ≈ ±0.5 % Temperature drift: ≈ 10–30 ppm/°C Low-voltage variants output a scaled current or a sub-1 V reference
  • Trim: the bandgap is trimmed first at wafer test (eFuse), because every later trim and measurement depends on it (see the test flow).
  • Two independent bandgaps: one for regulation, one for the monitors. If a single bandgap drifted, the LDO output and its UV/OV threshold would drift together and the fault would be invisible (a common-cause failure).
  • Start-up circuit: a bandgap has a stable zero-current state; a start-up circuit forces it out of that state at power-up, and is itself checked by the POR.

Power-up sequencing

  • Why the order matters: an input driven while its own supply is off can back-power the rail through ESD diodes; a core that starts before its IO can drive pins to undefined states; RF blocks enabled before the PLL locks radiate off-frequency.
  • Inrush: charging large decoupling capacitors at start-up draws a current spike; soft-start ramps each LDO to keep inrush and supply droop within the PMIC's limits.

Production test of the power system

  1. Power-rail short check before any supply ramps (see open/short).
  2. Bandgap trim against a precision tester voltage; then each LDO VOUT trim; burn eFuse and re-measure.
  3. Line and load regulation: step VIN and the load (on-chip load switches or tester PMU), measure VOUT.
  4. UV/OV threshold test: sweep each rail slowly with the tester supply and record the trip and release points (threshold and hysteresis); also verifies the deglitch time with short pulses.
  5. POR threshold and delay, over-current limit and over-temperature flag (forced via test mode).
  6. Quiescent current (IDDQ-like) of each LDO and the standby current; outliers go to PAT.
  7. Monitor self-test exercised the same way the firmware runs it at key-on.
