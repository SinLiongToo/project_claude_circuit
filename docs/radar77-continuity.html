Why and how: the ESD-diode method

First test

The first tests at every insertion (wafer probe and final test) check that the tester is actually connected to every pin, and that no pin is shorted, before any power is applied. The chip is not powered. The tester's per-pin PMU pushes a small current into each pin and reads the voltage across the pin's ESD protection diode. The test takes a few milliseconds, catches bad contacts, bent probes, bridged balls and assembly opens, and protects the part and the load board from powering up into a short.

Measuring contact resistance (Cres)

Kelvin (4-wire) method

Two needles touch the same pad: one carries the force current, the other only senses voltage (no current, so no drop across it). The difference between the force and sense voltages is the drop across the force contact:

R_c = (V_force − V_sense) / I_force I_force ≈ 10–50 mA (resolution), limited by pad/ESD rating

Needs Kelvin-capable pads or probes. Common for power and critical analog pins, and on FT sockets.

Two-current diode method (no Kelvin)

Force two currents through the same ESD diode. The diode's logarithmic term is known, so what remains is the series resistance (contact + pad + routing):

V(I) = n·V_T·ln(I/I_s) + I·R_s R_s = [ΔV − n·V_T·ln(I₂/I₁)] / (I₂ − I₁) e.g. I₁ = 100 µA, I₂ = 1 mA, n·V_T ≈ 30 mV → ln term ≈ 69 mV, the rest ÷ 0.9 mA = R_s

Works on every diode-protected pin with standard PMUs. Resolution is limited to about 1 Ω, which is enough to flag bad contacts.

04

Using the data, and where it sits in the flow

Using Cres data. Log Cres per site and per touchdown, not just pass/fail. A rising trend means the probe tips need cleaning, and the prober can trigger online cleaning automatically before contact failures become yield loss. Retesting "open" fails once after re-contact separates contact problems from real defects. Track the recovery rate, because a high one points to the probe card, not the wafer.

Where it sits in the test flow

  1. Cres (the tester's own contact check), then open/short on all pins in parallel, all unpowered.
  2. Power-rail short check, then ramp the supplies with current limits.
  3. Leakage (IIL/IIH) and IDDQ with the chip powered.
  4. Only then: scan, MBIST, BIST/BISM and everything else. Continuity failures are binned separately (contact vs device) so yield reports don't confuse probe-card problems with silicon defects.
Limits are typical values for planning. Actual windows come from characterising the ESD structures of your process and package over temperature, and from the tester and probe-card capability.
